About the workshop

Why SE4All, and why now

Software decides who can learn, work, bank, get care, and take part in public life. A large share of it still shuts out people with disabilities, older adults, and anyone facing a situational or cognitive barrier. At the same time, AI has moved into the heart of how software is built and used: it writes interface code, powers conversational agents, and increasingly makes decisions on a user's behalf. That is both a risk and an opening. The same models that can describe an image, caption a video, simplify dense text, or restructure a cluttered page are the tools that could close accessibility gaps at a scale that manual work never reached, but only if we engineer and evaluate them with that goal in mind.

SE4All treats this as a software engineering problem: how we test for accessibility, build tools and pipelines around it, share the work between people and AI, and hold the results to account. The workshop keeps its feet in SE methodology and practice while drawing on the accessibility, HCI, and AI communities. We especially welcome experience reports, practitioner talks, and contributions from engineers and researchers with lived experience of disability, whose perspective keeps the work grounded in the needs of the people it is meant to serve.

Topics of interest

Read broadly · a paper need not be about disability in the narrow sense

SE4All welcomes research, tools, experience, and vision across the full software lifecycle, wherever software engineering and AI meet accessibility and inclusion. Topics include, but are not limited to:

  • AI across the lifecycleGenerating, repairing, reviewing, and testing software, and what it means for accessibility and inclusion.
  • Human–AI collaborationHuman-in-the-loop workflows and crowdsourcing for building and maintaining better software.
  • Testing & analysisAutomated testing, auditing, and quality assurance for accessibility, usability, and inclusive design.
  • Process & designRequirements, architecture, design, and maintenance that treat inclusion as a first-class concern.
  • Empirical studiesMining repositories, datasets, and benchmarks for inclusive software engineering.
  • Emerging interfacesAccessibility of conversational agents, generated UIs, mobile, web, IoT, and smart environments.
  • Trust & fairnessVerification, dependability, fairness, and responsible engineering of AI-enabled software for diverse users.
  • Human & social aspectsDiversity, equity, standards and policy in practice (WCAG, EU Accessibility Act, Section 508), and education.
  • Experience reportsIndustrial case studies and lessons learned from building software for all.

Submissions

Two tracks · IEEE format · single-blind review

All submissions must be in PDF and follow the IEEE Conference Proceedings format (10 pt, IEEEtran). Each paper receives at least three reviews. Accepted papers appear in the ICSE 2027 Companion Proceedings, published by IEEE.

Up to 8 pages

Regular papers

Original research, empirical studies, and tool papers, including references.

Up to 5 pages

Short papers

Work in progress, position and vision papers, emerging ideas, tool demonstrations, and industrial experience reports, including references.
